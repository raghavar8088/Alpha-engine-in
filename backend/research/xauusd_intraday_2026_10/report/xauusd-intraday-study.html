Can an intraday gold strategy make 2% a trade?

11 published intraday families100 parameter sets in-sample 2010-01 → 2021-09out-of-sample 2021-10 → 2026-10-16 selection locked · code 6afbfebbaaa71054

Not reliably. Ten of the eleven intraday families fall far short of the 0.10% a trade needed: at their best in-sample settings none made more than +0.040% a trade after costs, and out of sample they made between −0.060% and +0.054%. One, a breakout straddle on US payroll Fridays, passes the test as written: +0.249% a trade out of sample, 2% of the account at about 8× leverage. But that result assumes stop orders fill exactly at their level in the minute the jobs report is released. Filled one minute later it makes +0.052% a trade, which would need about 39× leverage for 2%.

What 2% a trade means intraday. From 2010 gold's median session spanned 1.25% from high to low, and the COMEX day session (08:20–13:30 New York) only 0.84%; just 5% of COMEX day sessions spanned 2%. So 2% a trade can only come from leverage. This study allows up to 20× (the EU retail cap for gold CFDs, close to MCX gold futures margins). At 20×, 2% of the account needs +0.10% a trade on the position after costs. At 20×, a 5% move against the position wipes out the account. Out of sample, 9 of the other ten families lost 90% or more of a 20× account, and 3 went to zero in a single trade (Williams volatility breakout, day-trade form, 2026-02-06; Asia/London session drift, 2026-01-30; Floor-trader pivots, 2026-01-30).

This is the intraday companion to the position-trading study. Backtests on past prices; not a forecast.

The one pass, and why it does not hold up

Volatility · US payroll Fridays, 08:30 New York

US payrolls-day breakout

Rules. Straddle: OCO stops 0.02% beyond the 08:00-08:30 range from 08:30. Continuation: from 09:00 follow the 08:30-09:00 move. Locked parameters: mode=straddle, exit=end, tp=3R.

Stop-loss and take-profit. Stop at the far side of the range; target 3R or none; flat at 13:30 or 16:59.

Source. Practitioner rule (straddle the pre-release range / follow the first half-hour); release days by BLS's published schedule rule. No peer-reviewed gold study found.

Out of sample it made +0.249% a trade over 55 trades (profit factor 2.61, 55% winners), about $7 a trade on gold near $2,700. That is 2% of the account a trade at about 8× leverage. The same entries on random days averaged −0.026%, so the payroll release is what pays, and the same straddle on every other weekday lost money. In-sample it earned +0.123% a trade (t = 2.05), the only one of 100 configurations to clear the bar.

The catch. The test fills the stop order exactly at its level in the minute the jobs report comes out. Gold moves several dollars in that minute and spreads widen, so real stop orders fill further away. Filled at the close of that minute, or at the next minute's open, the out-of-sample average falls to +0.052% a trade and the in-sample average to −0.001%. In-sample, $1 of extra slippage a side already makes it a loser.

Why intraday rules fail on gold

  • Costs are large next to intraday moves. A round trip costs about $0.67 an ounce in spread, slippage and commission (base), 0.05% on gold at $1,300 and 0.02% at $3,500. Out of sample the ten other families captured −0.031% to +0.082% a trade before costs, so the costs take most or all of it.
  • Not enough edge to pay for them. At their best in-sample settings the closest families made Williams volatility breakout, day-trade form +0.040%, London breakout of the Asian range −0.005%, Asia/London session drift −0.021%, Opening-range breakout, first bar −0.023% a trade after costs, against the 0.10% needed. Out of sample, London breakout of the Asian range (p = 0.005 before correcting for 11 tests) beat the same trades placed at the same clock times on random days by more than chance would explain, yet made only +0.035% a trade. The rest did no better than random days.
  • Gold's intraday drift is small. The Asia/London overnight drift earned +0.05% a trade out of sample, mostly because gold rose. In-sample it lost, and at 20× it was wiped out in January 2026.
  • Opening-range and momentum rules from equities do not carry over. The Zarattini ORB and noise-area rules and Gao's half-hour momentum were built on US stock indexes. On gold they lose after costs in every period.

Data

One-minute bars: HistData.com XAUUSD M1, 2009-03-15 → 2026-10-16, 6,160,591 minutes in 4,543 sessions. Two checks against Dukascopy's hourly bid and ask changed how the data is used. HistData's prices are Dukascopy's bid quotes (median difference $0.000), so the feed is the same one at minute resolution, not an independent source. Its timestamps follow New York time with daylight saving (hourly returns match only at UTC−5 in winter and UTC−4 in summer, correlation 0.9997–1.0000). HistData's own notes say they are fixed at UTC−5, which would have shifted every session window by an hour for half of each year.

Mid prices = bid + half the spread Dukascopy quoted in that hour. The median spread by year was 2010: $0.51, 2011: $0.43, 2012: $0.36, 2013: $0.36, 2014: $0.30, 2015: $0.32, 2016: $0.31, 2017: $0.24, 2018: $0.24, 2019: $0.29, 2020: $0.41, 2021: $0.36, 2022: $0.37, 2023: $0.33, 2024: $0.38, 2025: $0.59, 2026: $0.69. Sessions roll at 17:00 New York, and October 2026 ends on the 16th, where HistData's file ends. The minute bars carry no volume, so rules that need VWAP were run without it.

Method

  • Split and lock. In-sample 2010-01-04 → 2021-09-30; out-of-sample from 2021-10-01. One configuration per family was chosen on in-sample data only, written to a lock file with a hash of the code, and evaluated out of sample once.
  • Selection. max in-sample t-stat among configs with >= min trades and an average >= +0.10% a trade (C1); at least 100 in-sample trades.
  • Fills. Decisions use only earlier minutes; market entries fill at the next minute's open; stop entries at their level (or a worse open); within one minute the worse outcome is assumed, a stop before a target; every position is closed by 16:59 New York.
  • Costs. Base: $0.40 spread, $0.10 slippage and $0.035 commission an ounce a side. Harsh: $0.50, $0.25 and $0.05. Measured: Dukascopy's quoted spread in the entry and exit hours. No financing, since nothing is held over the 17:00 roll.
  • Payroll days. Release dates follow BLS's published rule (the third Friday after the week containing the 12th), which reproduces every 2019 release. Releases delayed by government shutdowns (October 2013, autumn 2025) are not modelled; on those days the rule trades an ordinary Friday.
  • Stress tests of the pass were added after the locked run and change no verdict.

Assumptions and limitations

  • One broker feed (Dukascopy, via HistData). Another broker's spreads, especially around news, differ.
  • One-minute bars hide the order of prices inside a minute; the engine takes the worse outcome, but it cannot see the slippage of a stop order in a news minute. That is why the payroll straddle is stress-tested.
  • Leverage is applied as a constant multiple. A real account also faces margin calls, and many brokers widen spreads or halt trading around releases.
  • 100 configurations were tried. One clearing a t of about 2 in-sample is roughly what chance alone would produce, which is why the out-of-sample run and stress tests matter more than the in-sample result.

Code

Everything re-runs from backend/research/xauusd_intraday_2026_10: xi_fetch.py → xi_data.py → xi_run.py select → xi_run.py evaluate → xi_diagnostics.py → xi_report.py. The engine is xi_engine.py, and each rule is in xi_strategies.py. The rule that passed:

def nfp(D, C, days, mode, exit, tp):
    """US payrolls days (08:30 New York): straddle the 08:00-08:30 range with OCO stops, or follow the
    08:30-09:00 move from 09:00; stop at the far side; optional 3R target; flat at the exit time."""
    rows = []
    for d in days:
        if D["sess_date"][d] not in NFP:
            continue
        xl = last_before(D, d, T[exit])
        if xl is None:
            continue
        if mode == "straddle":
            ia, ib = at(D, d, T["ny0800"]), at(D, d, T["data"])
            if ia is None or ib is None:
                continue
            hi, lo = D["h"][ia:ib].max(), D["l"][ia:ib].min()
            buf = 0.0002 * hi
            f = stop_entry(D, ib, xl, hi + buf, lo - buf)
            if f is None:
                continue
            ei, side, ep, inside = f
            stop = lo - buf if side == 1 else hi + buf
        else:
            ia, ib = at(D, d, T["data"]), at(D, d, T["ny0900"])
            if ia is None or ib is None:
                continue
            move = D["c"][ib - 1] / D["o"][ia] - 1.0
            if move == 0:
                continue
            side = 1 if move > 0 else -1
            ei, ep, inside = ib, D["o"][ib], False
            stop = D["l"][ia:ib].min() if side == 1 else D["h"][ia:ib].max()
        risk = abs(ep - stop)
        if risk <= 0:
            continue
        tgt = ep + side * 3 * risk if tp == "3R" else np.nan
        xi, xp, why = run_exit(D, side, ei, ep, stop, tgt, xl, inside)
        rows.append((d, side, ei, ep, xi, xp, why))
    return rows
