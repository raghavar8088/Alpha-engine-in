Which published gold strategies make 2% a trade?

23 strategy families238 parameter sets tested in-sample 2010-01 → 2021-09out-of-sample 2021-10 → 2026-10-23 selection locked 2026-10-24 07:55 UTC · code 2e704d054e71ff37

1 of 23 strategies passes the 2%-a-trade test out of sample: Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen; Bartsch et al.). It does not time gold better than the same trades entered at random dates, and it does not beat simply holding gold on a risk-adjusted basis.

Holding gold returned +105% over the out-of-sample period after financing: +9% from October 2021 to December 2023 and +81% from January 2024. The same trades entered on random dates would have averaged +5.64%, so the pass comes from the period, not from the rule's timing. The checks under the strategy below separate the two.

Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen; Bartsch et al.) owes its average to one trade: 2023-12-01 to 2026-07-01, +84.3%. Its other 11 trades averaged −3.15%, and its median trade was −2.48%.

Two later checks. On 2005–2009, years no stage had used, 2 of the locked choices clear 2% a trade (Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen; Bartsch et al.) +2.55% against +4.05% for random entries, p = 0.66; Moving-average crossover (Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron; golden cross) +6.44% against +8.55% for random entries, p = 0.72). In a walk-forward from 2014 that re-picks each year's parameters from earlier years only, 1 family passes (Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen; Bartsch et al.) +2.88% against +1.24% for random entries, p = 0.21). None of these results is better than random timing by a margin that rules out luck.

These are backtests on past prices. They do not predict future returns, and every losing year and drawdown is shown below. "Per trade" means the unlevered move a trade captured after all costs, on the whole account in one position; leverage would multiply it, risk included.

Why fewer than three pass, and why the search stopped

The brief asked for the search to continue until three strategies pass. It stopped at 1. Any rule added from here would be designed after seeing the out-of-sample answer: that from October 2021, rules that stayed long through the 2024–26 rally cleared 2% a trade and almost nothing else did. A new family, a looser trade minimum or a re-tuned parameter chosen with that knowledge would pass because of it, and its out-of-sample result would no longer test anything. The two checks in the next section use data and a procedure that avoid that problem.

What was tried. 23 families with published rules and 238 parameter sets: trend-following (monthly, weekly and daily), breakouts and volatility systems with stops, mean reversion, London-session and overnight rules, and calendar seasonals. Each was tested long-only and long/short where the source allows, under four cost sets, with entries at the next open or by stop orders.

Why they failed. Gold rarely moves 2% in a session, so the rules that hold for hours or days peaked at −0.02% to +0.98% a trade in-sample. The daily and weekly trend systems that trade more often (Turtle breakout, Clenow core trend model, Keltner / ATR channel breakout, Breakout with LeBeau chandelier exit, Wilder DMI / ADX trend, MACD(12,26,9) trend, Ichimoku cloud trend, Weinstein stage analysis) reached +0.55% to +1.99% a trade at their best in-sample settings; 2010–2021 held gold's long fall from 2011 and the sideways years to 2019. The slow rules (Faber monthly SMA timing, Winter effect, Gold monthly moving-average rules, Moving-average crossover) did clear 2% a trade, but they trade once or twice a year: five out-of-sample years gave them 3 to 6 trades each, below the minimum of 8. In 4 of the 4, the single best trade supplied more than half the profit.

Why session and mean-reversion rules cannot get there. From 2010 the median session spanned 1.25% from high to low; 18% of sessions had a range of 2% or more, and 5% moved 2% from open to close. Rules that are flat by the end of a session or two, like the London breakout, the session drift, Williams and Crabel breakouts, RSI(2), Double 7s and band reversion, would need nearly every trade to catch most of a day's range to average 2%. The London breakout caps its target at 2.5R with a stop of at most 0.6%, so no trade can make more than 1.5%. In this test only rules that hold for months averaged 2% a trade, and they trade so rarely that five years gave them three to six trades.

Method

  • Split. In-sample 2010-01-01 → 2021-09-30 (about 70%), out-of-sample 2021-10-01 → 2026-10-23 (about 30%). Each family's parameters were chosen on in-sample data only, then written to a lock file with a hash of the code. The out-of-sample stage refuses to run if the code changes after the lock, and it was run once.
  • Selection. max in-sample t-stat among configs with >= min trades and avg >= target (costs C1); at least 12 in-sample trades. Of 23 families, 6 had at least one configuration that qualified.
  • No look-ahead. Decisions are taken at a session's close (~17:00 New York) from bars up to that close, and acted on in the next session: market orders at the next hourly open, breakout orders as stop orders that fill at the level (or at a worse open). Stops, targets and stop entries are checked on hourly bars. When a stop and a target fall in the same hour, the stop is assumed hit first, and a stop touched in the hour a trade opened counts against it.
  • Prices. Mid prices from Dukascopy's bid and ask. Daily sessions roll at 17:00 New York, the standard for spot metals.
  • Sizing. Every strategy is tested at 1× notional, one position at a time, compounding, so its per-trade return is directly the percentage it captured. Published sizing rules (Turtle units, volatility scaling, 1% risk) are listed with each strategy but not applied.
  • Diagnostics. Harsh costs; a random-entry test (5,000 sets of the strategy's own trades with the same side and holding length, entered at random dates in the same period); a 10,000-draw bootstrap of the out-of-sample trades; the 2021–23 vs 2024–26 split; neighbouring parameter sets; holding gold under the same costs. Timing p-values are corrected with Holm's method across the strategies tested.

Spread is paid once a round trip (half on each side), slippage and commission on each side; commission $0.035/oz a side is $7 a 100-oz lot round trip. Financing is charged a night for every calendar day held, as CFD swaps are (T-bill from Yahoo ^IRX). Held cash earns nothing.

Assumptions and limitations

  • One bank's quotes (Dukascopy). Another broker's prices and spreads differ, most in fast markets.
  • Hourly bars hide the order of prices inside an hour; the engine takes the worse outcome each time, which may understate stop-and-target strategies slightly.
  • Financing follows a CFD-style model (T-bill plus a markup). On futures the carry is paid through the roll, and it is similar in size. Leaving financing out (cost set C0) flatters long trades, by more the longer they are held.
  • Out-of-sample is one regime: gold rose about 105% in five years. Long-biased rules are favoured by it, and the 2021–23 vs 2024–26 columns show how much.
  • Monthly and weekly rules trade a few times a year. Their averages rest on few trades, so the bootstrap interval is wide.
  • 238 parameter sets across 23 families were tried. The more that are tried, the more likely a pass is luck. The random-entry test and Holm's correction address part of this; parameter neighbours address the rest.
  • Rules were coded from their published descriptions. Deviations are listed per strategy. The Turtle System 1 filter is a daily-bar approximation, and Clenow's rules come from summaries of the book.
  • Taxes, position limits, margin calls and broker outages are not modelled.

Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen; Bartsch et al.) (xa_strategies.py)

def tsmom(m: Market, lookback: int, direction: str) -> Plan:
    me = month_end_mask(m)
    idx = np.flatnonzero(me)
    mc = m.d_c[idx]
    # excess over the T-bill (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen use excess returns); the bill accrues on calendar days
    rate = m.rate
    cum = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(rate.values / 365.0)])
    ri = rate.index.get_indexer(pd.to_datetime(m.d_date[idx]))
    sig = np.zeros(len(idx))
    for j in range(lookback, len(idx)):
        past = mc[j] / mc[j - lookback] - 1.0
        bill = cum[ri[j]] - cum[ri[j - lookback]]
        ex = past - bill
        sig[j] = 1.0 if ex > 0 else (-1.0 if direction == "long_short" else 0.0)
    full = np.full(len(me), np.nan)
    full[idx] = sig
    return state_plan(ffill_state(full, me))
