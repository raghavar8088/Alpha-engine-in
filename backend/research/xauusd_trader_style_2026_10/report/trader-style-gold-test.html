Do the profitable traders' rules work when coded?

3 setups24 parameter setschosen on gold 2010-01 → 2021-09 then run unchanged on silver, EUR/USD, GBP/USDrules registered before any result · code 76f1dda2c224c8d9
No. The rules were coded the way successful gold traders describe them:
  • risk 1% of the account per trade with a fixed stop;
  • target 2–3 times the risk;
  • one setup at a fixed time (the London open or the New York open);
  • at most one trade a day, and only in the direction of the daily trend.
On gold 2010–2021 the best setting of each setup still lost money after costs, and the same rules lost on silver, EUR/USD and GBP/USD. Only gold 2021–2026, a strong bull market, showed a small profit, below the bar set in advance.

Why. The setups are slightly better than chance before costs: +0.00R to +0.14R a trade. Costs take 0.04R to 0.43R a trade, because the stops are tight and a fixed cost is a large share of a small stop. Risk control decides how fast an account wins or loses; it cannot make a setup with no edge after costs profitable. (R = the amount risked on the trade.)

What this rules in, and out

  • Ruled out: trend-aligned session breakouts and pullbacks with fixed stops and 2–3R targets, as commonly taught, on gold, silver, EUR/USD and GBP/USD, at ordinary retail costs.
  • Not tested: the trader's judgment, meaning which days to skip, how to read the news and how to manage a trade by feel. That can only be tested by keeping a journal of real or paper trades over months.
  • One lead: costs were smallest in R where the stops were widest (gold 2021–26, stops of about $11–18). A setup that trades less often with wider stops faces a lower cost hurdle. It would still need a real edge before costs of at least +0.2R.
