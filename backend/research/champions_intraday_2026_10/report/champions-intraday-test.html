Do the champions' intraday rules work?

7 rules332 settings tried in-sampleone setting locked per rule and market out of sample run oncerules written down first · code 9d807cc9aad5ffd1

On gold, no. All seven rules lost money or broke even after costs out of sample, as in every earlier gold study. On the stock-index futures these traders built the rules for, the breakouts show a small edge. No single rule is strong enough to pass on its own, but run together the way Unger runs his systems, the 14 rules that were profitable in-sample made +0.25% a month out of sample at 1× (t = 2.8), with every calendar year positive. That is about 3% a year without leverage, and nowhere near 2% a trade.

What 2% a trade means here. A day trade on gold or an index moves well under 2%, so 2% is only reachable on the account, with leverage. At 20×, the most the study allows, 2% of the account needs +0.10% a trade on the position after costs. The best single rule made +0.083% a trade out of sample (Nasdaq 100, williams oops! gap reversal) but only +0.055% in-sample, and at 10× it fell −50% at its worst.

A rule passes on its own only with: a profit in-sample; at least 50 trades out of sample, profitable at base and double costs; a positive mean that survives the correction for 28 tests; and better timing than the same trades on random days. None does. The closest are breakouts on the Nasdaq and the DAX (t ≈ 2, before the correction).

Unger's way: run them together

Unger runs many simple systems at once, so that a bad month for one is a good month for another. The portfolio fixed in advance takes every rule that made money in-sample: 14 rules (Gold crabel breakout after a narrow day, S&P 500 crabel breakout after a narrow day, S&P 500 williams volatility breakout, S&P 500 unger first-hours breakout, Nasdaq 100 crabel breakout, every day, Nasdaq 100 crabel breakout after a narrow day, Nasdaq 100 williams volatility breakout, Nasdaq 100 williams oops! gap reversal, Nasdaq 100 unger first-hours breakout, DAX crabel breakout, every day, DAX crabel breakout after a narrow day, DAX williams volatility breakout, DAX williams oops! gap reversal, DAX unger first-hours breakout). Each gets an equal share of the account.

The portfolio's return each month out of sample, at 1×. 59% of months were up; the best was 2025-04 (+3.1%), the tariff shock.

Checks on the result

  • Not the market's own rise. Buying every session's open and selling its close on the same markets made +0.086% a month (t +0.28).
  • The days matter. The same trades moved to random days lost −0.048% a month on average; none of 2,000 random versions did as well (p < 0.001).
  • Not one lucky month. Without its best month the t-statistic is 2.58; without its best three, 2.11.
  • Costs. Still significant at double the futures costs (t 2.10); at three times, not (t 1.42); at six times it loses.
  • Weaker before. In-sample the same portfolio made +0.13% a month, about half the out-of-sample figure, with a −6% drawdown at 1×.

Gold

Every rule was given its best in-sample setting on gold's COMEX day session (08:20–13:30 New York) and then lost or broke even after costs out of sample. Gold's one in-sample winner, Crabel's breakout after an inside day, made +0.063% a trade in 2010–2021 and −0.063% after. Gold alone as a portfolio lost −0.08% a month. The earlier gold study, on the 18:00–17:00 day, found the same for Crabel's breakout (−0.005% a trade) and Williams' breakout (+0.017% a trade, a 20× account wiped out on 2026-02-06).

The cause is the same as before: at retail costs a round trip takes $0.67 an ounce, and these rules' gross edge on gold is smaller than that. Even at COMEX futures costs ($0.35) the best gold rule made +0.017% a trade.

"Timing p" is the share of 2,000 random placements (same side, entry minute and holding time on random days; for the bias rule, a random minute on the same days) that did as well. The bias rules' "start +N" is minutes after the open.

Not tested, and why

  • Paul Rotter scalped German bond futures for 3–5 ticks a trade by reading the order book, at an exchange member's costs. One-minute bars carry no order book and no queue position. On gold, a $0.40 retail spread is already 4 COMEX ticks, about the size of his whole target.
  • The forex-contest winners have not published their methods.
  • Michael Cook and Williams' original rules hold 2–5 days, so they are not intraday. Williams' swing breakout was tested in the position study and failed.

Data and limits

  • Source. HistData one-minute bars, free. Their clocks are New York time with daylight saving in every market (checked at each session's opening and data-release minutes).
  • Defects found and cut out before any strategy ran: February–July 2023 in every market (about half the minutes missing); the DAX file from June 2020 to December 2023, which holds Euro Stoxx 50 prices; WTI crude, which has no data for 2024 to May 2026 and jumps at contract rolls.
  • Gold's out-of-sample years are not unseen. Two earlier studies used them. The index markets are new to this repository.
  • Fills are modelled, worst case inside each minute, at futures-like costs for the indices and retail CFD costs for gold. No order of this study was ever placed.
  • The DAX's out-of-sample window is short: December 2023 to October 2026.
  • For a resident of India, money sent abroad under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme cannot be used for margin trading, so these US and German futures are out of reach. The Indian equivalent, NIFTY and BANKNIFTY futures, was not part of this test.
