## 1. Core Methodology — Supply & Demand Zones
*Source: [Full Price Action Course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lNmBBd2MIhU)*

This is the foundation every trade on the channel is built on.

**How a zone is formed (marking rules):**
- A zone is any level where the market **stalled or reversed** — where "some power" held or pushed price.
- On 15-min TF (intraday): after a move up, if the **next 4 candles fail to break the high** (either drop away or hold flat), mark a zone at that level. Same inverted for lows.
- **Recency rule:** only use recent swings — 2-3 days old max for intraday (15m). An 8-10 day old support is "not valid." For swing trades use 1H/4H recent swings.
- **Validity filter (30-40% rule):** a reversal point only counts as a zone if the counter-move retraced **at least 30-40%** of the prior leg (a 5% dip after a 100% leg is not a zone), **OR** the market spent meaningful **time** there (a 2-hour move needs ~30-40 min of consolidation at the level).

**How zones are traded (touch-count rules):**
| Touch | Action | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 1st touch | Fade it (sell at supply / buy at demand) | Full quantity (e.g. 2 lots) |
| 2nd touch | Fade again | **Half quantity** (1 lot) |
| 3rd touch | Do NOT fade — watch price action; if the level holds, **trade the breakout** instead | — |

- **Liquidity grab entry:** if price sweeps above a zone and instantly rejects, sell with SL at the sweep high — this is how he catches exact day-highs/day-lows.
- Trend filter: only fade zones **with** the higher-TF trend (downtrend + pullback into resistance zone = short; uptrend + support = long).
- Works identically on Nifty, BankNifty, Sensex, gold, BTC.
- He explicitly rejects SMC/ICT complexity ("order block, this block, that block — no need"): zones + risk-reward is the whole system ([Day 06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CB1FA0xnJ7A)).

**The math that makes it work:** risk ₹3,000 to make ₹6,000-12,000 (trail). At 1:4 RR you can lose 6 of 10 trades and still net +₹10 per ₹1 risked on winners.

## 2. The Option Buying Challenge System (Nifty 50 Scalping, 2 Lots)
*Source: 23-episode "Live Option Buying Challenge" series, [Day 01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bqFFSaYZnjg) → [Day 23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w5LcPFWpw2E), incl. your linked [Day 03](https://youtu.be/LPJXdaxfB5c)*

The complete rulebook he follows on camera every day:

**Position & instrument**
- Fixed **2 lots Nifty (~130-150 qty)**; 3-4 lots only on rare max-conviction setups.
- Buys ~**₹150 premium** strikes normally. On low-confidence days he buys **~₹100 premium** with a tighter SL instead of cutting lots ("reduce premium, not conviction").

**Timing rules (very strict)**
- **No morning trades.** He waits for the second half; preferred entry window **1:30-2:00 PM**. Reason: morning volatility is untradeable for beginners and the real trend shows after lunch.
- **Exit everything by ~3:00 PM** — after 3 PM premiums decay hard (theta), so option buys are never held into close. (Option *selling* positions benefit at that hour instead.)

**Entry logic**
1. Determine higher-TF trend (his "big picture" bias, often stated on his live streams the prior evening).
2. Wait for pullback INTO a zone in the trend direction: downtrend + pullback to resistance/selling zone → **buy puts**; uptrend + support tap → **buy calls**.
3. **Confirm with the premium chart itself:** if the spot rises 80 points but the call premium is flat/struggling ("suppressed"), calls are a trap — skip. If put premiums fell in the morning and are now consolidating, that's the put-side setup.
4. **Never trade the middle of a range** — "maximum trades there are trappy" (Day 03). Only act at range edges/zones.

**Risk rules**
- SL = day high (for put buys) or below the zone; cash risk **₹2,000-4,000 per trade** on 2 lots (~₹2,500 on low-confidence days).
- Target **1:2 minimum** (₹5,000-8,000), trail beyond if momentum.
- **Max 2 trades/day** (2-3 only on strongly trending days). One SL on a bad day → close the terminal.
- No revenge trading, no FOMO re-entries — "the market gives opportunity daily; come back tomorrow."

**Market-behavior playbook used in the series**
- **Gap-down trap (Day 03):** on a big gap-down (~300 pts), retailers who short at open get trapped by an immediate recovery rally in the first half; the real selloff comes in the second half, closing near day-low. So: let the morning trap play out, short the 1:30-2:00 failure.
- **Stop-hunt logic:** before reversing, the market sweeps the previous swing where retail SLs cluster; he plans entries on/after that sweep, not before.
- **Buyer/seller strength reading:** a market grinding up slowly with shrinking 1H green candles = buyer exhaustion → when the fall comes it is fast and pullback-less (sometimes justifying a slightly early entry). A rejection candle at a supply area must fall *instantly* — if it holds sideways 1-2 candles, it will break up instead.
- **Mid-range / Fib 0.5 ([Day 18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YZHlP2Ng0t4)):** the 50% level of the day's big leg is where sellers must defend for the move to be "real."
- **Expiry-day round numbers ([Day 21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzk4_cv_X1U)):** at round strikes (24,000/24,500) on expiry, expect a sweep of the day-high followed by rejection — option sellers pile in on the first red candle there.

## 3. Option Selling System (his main money-maker)
*Sources: [Option Selling Strategy Exposed | 25K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VEWpdKQJWlg) · [3.7L Profit Full Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eaIuQ-gtYZk) · [Selling Special Day 19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RRkBg1ytgzg) · [Day 21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gzk4_cv_X1U)*

His stated edge: **"premiums are born to go to zero"** — so he shorts premium spikes, never chases them.

### 3a. Premium Pullback Short (the ₹3.7L trade)
1. Build a **directional view** from structure: character change (market that bought every dip starts selling every rally), M-pattern top = lower high + previous swing low broken, plus macro overlay (e.g., US oil tapping a resistance 3rd time → risk-off).
2. Wait for the option premium (the side you want to short) to **pull back 40-85% upward** of its prior fall — "premium ne pullback liya" is the golden setup when your view agrees.
3. Scale in on the pullback; add **maximum quantity right after a big 1H red candle closes** on the premium chart; SL = that candle's high (tiny SL vs. large position).
4. Carry overnight only when sitting on a healthy open profit. He captured **247 of Nifty's 248-point fall** this way — his account-growth philosophy is "capture the big points, not 10-point scalps."
5. Expect one ₹3L-class winner every 1-2 months; in between, take small ₹3k-10k losses without flinching.

### 3b. Intraday OTM Selling (beginner template, Day 19)
- When no clean directional setup exists: sell a **far/slightly OTM** option *against* the exhausted move, with a cheap far-OTM buy (₹1-12) as **hedge** to reduce margin.
- Entry trigger: premium **spike** into a marked resistance zone ("I need the spike — that's what I sell").
- Sizing formula: max profit = premium × qty (e.g. ₹40 × 65 = ₹2,600). **SL = half of max profit** (₹1,300 ≈ 20 premium points) → built-in 1:2.
- Never sell the side the market is running toward. To sell puts (bullish view), wait for a dip to support with put premiums pumping; to sell calls, wait for a 100-300 point one-way rally into resistance.
- **POP logic:** a ₹50 premium must gain +100% to hit a 100%-away SL — the exact move option buyers almost never catch is your stop, so 60-70% win rate is realistic (1:1 RR is acceptable in OTM selling only, unlike buying).
- He sold OTM exclusively for his first 2 years of selling; ATM/expiry selling came later.

### 3c. Expiry-day tactics (Day 21)
- Sell **ATM at a round-number strike** after the day-high is swept and rejected (buyers' breakouts fail there; sellers re-enter on the first red candle).
- Small SL (e.g. 10 points on a ₹30 premium, risking ₹500 for ₹2,000), and **re-enter once** if stopped and the premium spikes back to the level.
- Known expiry pattern: ₹20-40 OTM premiums spike to ₹120-150 in minutes then dump — never panic, never carry shorts on the **most crowded round strike** (e.g. 24,000) to zero; book at ₹5-10.
- 3 PM+: decay accelerates — selling is favored, buying is dead.

## 4. The Four Chart Patterns He Trades
*Source: [Pattern Every Profitable Trader Knows Ep. 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T2RG0Fn_t0Q) & [Ep. 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fc9o39iHrII)*

He states he trades **only these four patterns** ("5 saal me saare indicators laga ke dekh liye — price action hi god hai").

**Pattern 1 — Range Breakout + Retest** (trending entries)
- Find a range on 1H+ (higher TF = higher accuracy). **The longer the range, the more money trapped inside, the bigger the move** (example: 73-day Nifty consolidation).
- Note which edge price *sustains* against — that's the likely break side. Direct instant breakouts are usually failures; good breakouts sustain or false-break first.
- On break: shift down to 15m/5m for a smaller SL; do NOT buy the first pullback — wait for the pullback leg itself to break in the breakout direction (a red candle taking out the last green candle's low, for shorts).
- SL above last swing, target 1:2 for beginners (1:3-1:5 once consistent), then trail.
- After a long-range breakout, keep re-entering on every pullback-leg break for 2-3 days — expect a 1,000-3,000 point Nifty move after a 70-day range.

**Pattern 2 — Flag & Pole** (trend continuation)
- In a trending market: impulse (pole) → pullback (flag) → draw a trendline on the pullback → enter on trendline break (or retest), SL below last swing (the "U-turn" candle), target previous high / 1:2-1:3.
- Psychology: profit-booking + counter-trend sellers create the flag; trend resumption runs their stops.
- Prerequisite: the market must already have finished its sideways + volatile phases — the 3 phases cycle is **sideways → volatile → trending** (market is sideways ~70% of the time).

**Pattern 3 — M Pattern at Supply Zone** (range-bound shorts)
- Inside an established multi-day range: at the supply (top) edge, drop to 15m and hunt the **M shape**: high → pullback → lower high → break of the previous swing low.
- Entry on the red candle closing that break; SL just above the supply zone; targets 1:2 up to 1:4-1:5 (range bottom). Expect 1-2 stops before the payoff trade; re-enter after liquidity-grab candles.

**Pattern 4 — W Pattern at Demand Zone** (range-bound longs)
- Mirror image at the range bottom: low → bounce → higher low (previous low NOT broken) → break of the interim high (the candle that runs the shorts' stops = entry candle).
- SL below the W's second low, target 1:2 then the supply edge.
- "Don't memorize the letters — read the candles": the pattern is just structure-shift at the zone.

## 5. Liquidity Sweep Strategy ("sniper entries")
*Source: [What's LIQUIDITY in SMC, Explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uxkMQ7MH55A)*

- Concept: institutions can only fill big sell orders where buy orders cluster — so they engineer moves INTO liquidity (equal highs, multi-touch levels, trendlines, range extremes) before reversing.
- **The 3-step setup:**
  1. **Identify the liquidity zone** — equal highs / a level tapped many times / a 4-5+ day range / a 2-3 day respected trendline. (He skips sweeps of ordinary single swings — only levels where *time was spent* matter.)
  2. **Wait for the sweep** — price breaks the level, triggers the stops... and comes back inside the range. Never trade the breakout itself.
  3. **Enter on confirmation** — re-entry into range + **market structure shift** (first lower-high, previous swing low broken). SL just above the sweep's high. Target 1:2+, often the opposite side of the range.
- Trendline variant: in an uptrend, a wick that pierces the trendline and closes back above = long entry with SL below the wick.
- He claims this filter takes a 30-50% accuracy trader to 80-90% on these specific trades. (His BTC example: 2-month range, fake breakout up, then a 20,000-36,000 point dump.)

## 6. BTST (Buy Today Sell Tomorrow) Rules
*Source: [3 Lakh+ BTST Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sO5RXkEdbOU)*

- **Never carry overnight on hope. Only carry when the intraday position is already in solid profit** — you risk the day's profit, never fresh capital. (His example: +₹30k intraday cushion on a short 23400 CE carried into a 100-point gap-down = ₹1.5L.)
- Direction filter: carry **with the close** — market closing at/near day high on a strong green daily candle → carry longs; deep red close near day-low → carry shorts.
- The 2×2 outcome grid (big profit / small profit / small loss / big loss): the profit cushion + close-direction filter deletes the "big loss" cell.
- Explicitly **not for beginners** and never while in drawdown.
- For option sellers, carrying also needs a points cushion: only hold a short from ₹250 if it already trades ₹150-180; never carry a barely-profitable short (20-30 pts).

## 7. Risk & Money Management (the part he calls 80% of the game)
*Source: [Risk Management Strategy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0uHk9vj-7Xg) + repeated across series*

**Losing-streak protocol (5 steps):**
1. **Cut lot size in half** (beginners: to a quarter — 2000 qty → 500). First goal is breaking the streak psychologically, not recovering money.
2. **Take a 5-7 day break** — don't open the broker at all; study charts only. Revenge mode is how accounts evaporate. Never top up capital to "recover."
3. **Audit your trading journal** — was the streak the strategy, or emotions? (His own finding: after 2-3 losses he starts cutting winners early and exiting before SL is hit — manufactured losses.)
4. **Never switch strategies mid-streak** — a setup needs 50-100 trades to show its real accuracy; every system has a market phase it loses in (a trend system bleeds in a 7-day range, then pays it all back on the 5,000-point move).
5. **Reset the mindset** — return with the exact same per-trade risk system (e.g. ₹3k risk → ₹6-9k reward). Split recovery into small chunks (₹50k loss = 5 × ₹10k wins), never one hero trade.

**Standing rules across all videos**
- RR below 1:2 → the trade doesn't exist (exception: 1:1 allowed only in OTM option selling because POP is on your side).
- Higher RR targets = lower hit rate; beginners start at 1:2 for psychological wins, scale to 1:4-1:10 later (one 1:10 catch funds the next 10 free attempts).
- Quantity scales with confluence: full size only when **zone + trend + view** all align; half size / smaller premium / tighter SL when anything is missing; split entries with spacing when unsure.
- SL is non-negotiable: "Ignore the stop loss and trading will ignore you." Analysis wrong → exit, re-analyze, re-enter elsewhere; the market runs on probability, never certainty.
- Account growth comes from capturing multi-hundred-point moves at 1:4+, not from daily scalp dopamine.

## 8. Psychology & Meta-Rules
*Sources: [Trading Psychology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bhwg7B3NG2s) · [5 Beginner Mistakes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9h6AN12aPgI) · scattered across the series*

- Strategy is ~20% of profitability; psychology + money management is the rest. "Give the same setup to a profitable and a losing trader — the results will still differ."
- The killer pattern: a few green days → overconfidence → projecting future income → first red streak → revenge-risk the entire previous profit → capital gone. Recover in small chunks only.
- After big profit, he pre-decides how much of it he's allowed to give back (₹3L profit → will tolerate back to ₹1L, aiming ₹5L highs vs ₹2L max loss).
- Judge any educator by **6-12 months of P&L**, never a viral screenshot — he shows monthly Zerodha-style consoles on camera.
- Option buying is structurally harder than selling (his own ₹12L loss year was an option-buying year); he treats buying as the challenge/content format and selling as the income engine.
- Discipline artifacts in the series: fixed lots, fixed windows (1:30-3:00 PM), daily loss cap, day closed after target/SL.
